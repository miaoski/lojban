## 1. Vocabulary to Learn

**8 new gismu:**

- **spano** — x1 is Spanish/Hispanic in aspect x2
- **xindo** — x1 is Hindi/Indic in aspect x2
- **rusko** — x1 is Russian in aspect x2
- **xrabo** — x1 is Arabic in aspect x2
- **bangu** — x1 is a language used by x2 to express x3
- **valsi** — x1 is a word meaning x2 in language x3
- **smuni** — x1 is the meaning/semantics of x2 to interpreter x3

## 2. Grammar to Learn

### Comprehensive Compound Word Review

**Tanru (two-word compounds):**
- Basic form: modifier + modified (blanu gerku = blue dog)
- Place structure from rightmost word
- Free grouping unless bound with bo or ke...ke'e

**Lujvo (frozen tanru):**
- Built from rafsi (short forms of gismu)
- Example: jbobau (Lojban-language) from jbo + bau (rafsi of bangu)
- Permanently fixed compound with stable meaning
- Drill: gismu → find rafsi → combine → lujvo

**Fu'ivla (borrowed words):**
- Must contain consonant cluster
- Must end in vowel
- Pause before cmevla-like fu'ivla
- Example: tcati, kurki, kafke

**`co` (inversion):**
- Reverses tanru order
- Place structure from word BEFORE co
- Useful for long chains

### This Week's Drills:
1. Build tanru, then freeze into lujvo using rafsi
2. Decompose lujvo back into component gismu
3. Create fu'ivla from foreign words (especially language/culture terms)
4. Practice recognizing word boundaries in connected speech

## 5. Conversation Topics

**Topic:** Discuss languages and words

**Practice scenarios:**
1. "I speak English and Lojban" — `mi tavla bau la .glibau. .e la .jbobau.`
2. Name 5 languages using lujvo (junbau, spanbau, xilbau, rusbau, rabyrbau)
3. Discuss word meanings: "This word means X"
4. Create culture-specific fu'ivla

**Example sentences:**
- `mi tavla bau la .glibau. .e la .jbobau.` — I speak in English and Lojban
- `ti valsi zo prami la .jbobau.` — This word is "prami" in Lojban
- `lu prami li'u smuni lu love li'u mi` — "prami" means "love" to me
- `la .paris. cu junbau bangu` — Paris is a Chinese language (if discussing a Chinese dialect)
- `mi djuno lo smuni be ti bei mi` — I know the meaning of this to me
