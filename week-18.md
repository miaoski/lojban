# Week 18 — Attitudinals & Evidentials

## 1. Vocabulary to Learn

**No new gismu this week — Focus on 15 cmavo:**

### Attitudinals (emotional expressions):
- **.ui** — happiness/joy
- **.uinai** — unhappiness/sadness
- **.uu** — pity/compassion
- **.u'i** — amusement
- **.oi** — pain/complaint
- **.a'o** — hope/desire for outcome
- **.au** — desire/want
- **.e'e** — competence suggestion/encouragement
- **.ie** — agreement
- **.ienai** — disagreement

### Evidentials (how you know):
- **ba'a** — I expect (anticipation/expectation)
- **ti'e** — I hear (hearsay)
- **za'a** — I observe (direct observation)
- **pe'i** — I opine (opinion)
- **ju'o** — certainly (certainty about truth)

## 2. Grammar to Learn

### Attitudinals Express Emotions
- **Attitudinals are NOT part of the bridi** — they don't affect truth conditions
- **Placement:** Can go anywhere in the sentence (float freely)
- **What they modify:** Usually the word/phrase immediately following, or the whole bridi
- **Examples:**
  - `.ui mi klama` — (Happy!) I go
  - `mi .ui klama` — I (happily) go
  - `mi klama .ui` — I go (yay!)

### Intensity Scale
Attitudinals have intensity modifiers:
- **cai** — extreme intensity: `.uicai` (ecstatic)
- **sai** — strong intensity: `.uisai` (very happy)
- *(no modifier)* — normal: `.ui` (happy)
- **ru'e** — weak intensity: `.uiru'e` (slightly happy)
- **cu'i** — neutral/indifferent: `.uicu'i` (not caring either way)
- **nai** — opposite: `.uinai` (unhappy/sad)

### Combining Intensity and Negation
- `.uisai` — very happy
- `.uiru'e` — slightly happy
- `.uinai` — unhappy (opposite)
- `.uinaisai` — very unhappy
- `.uicu'i` — indifferent to happiness

### Evidentials Say How You Know
- **ba'a** — I expect it (based on prior knowledge)
- **ti'e** — I heard it (hearsay/someone told me)
- **za'a** — I observe it (I see/perceive it directly)
- **pe'i** — in my opinion (subjective judgment)
- **ju'o** — I'm certain (high confidence)

### Attitudinals Replace Tone of Voice
- In spoken language, we use tone/inflection to convey emotion
- In Lojban, attitudinals make emotion EXPLICIT
- Example: "I'm going to the store." (said sarcastically) → `mi klama lo zarci .ianai` (I go to the store [disagreement with the idea])

## 3. Vocabulary to Refresh

**All prior weeks (~138 gismu)** — Focus on:
- Common actions to attach emotions to: klama, citka, penmi, gunka
- States that trigger emotions: gleki, badri, terpa

## 4. Grammar to Refresh

- **Bridi structure:** Attitudinals don't change bridi truth, just add emotion
- **Scope:** Understanding what an attitudinal modifies
- **Placement flexibility:** Attitudinals can go almost anywhere

## 5. Conversation Topics

**Topic:** React to situations with appropriate emotional and evidential markers

**Practice scenarios:**
1. React to good news: `.ui sai` (very happy!)
2. React to bad news: `.uu` (pity/sympathy)
3. Express opinions with evidentials: `pe'i ti xamgu` (in my opinion, this is good)
4. Report hearsay: `ti'e la .tam. cu klama` (I heard that Tom goes)
5. Express certainty: `ju'o ti jetnu` (certainly this is true)

**Example sentences:**
- `.ui mi pu penmi la .alis.` — (Happy!) I met Alice
- `mi pu penmi la .alis. .ui sai` — I met Alice (very happy about it!)
- `.uu do terpa` — (Pity!) You're afraid
- `.u'i ti` — (Amused!) This (is funny)
- `pe'i ti xamgu` — In my opinion, this is good
- `za'a do klama` — I observe that you're going
- `ti'e la .tam. cu snada` — I heard that Tom succeeded
- `ju'o ti jetnu` — Certainly this is true
- `.a'o do snada` — (Hope!) You succeed / I hope you succeed

## Day 1 — Basic Attitudinals (15 min)

- Learn .ui, .uinai, .uu, .u'i, .oi
- Practice: `.ui mi klama` (Happy! I go)
- Practice: `.uu do terpa` (Pity! You're afraid)
- Practice: `.u'i ti` (Amusing! This)
- Drill: Add appropriate attitudinals to 5 neutral sentences

## Day 2 — Desire & Agreement Attitudinals (15 min)

- Learn .a'o, .au, .e'e, .ie, .ienai
- Practice: `.au mi citka` (I want to eat)
- Practice: `.ie do drani` (I agree, you're correct)
- Practice: `.a'o do snada` (I hope you succeed)
- Drill: Express desires and agreements in 5 scenarios

## Day 3 — Intensity Modifiers (15 min)

- Learn cai, sai, ru'e, cu'i, nai
- Practice: `.uicai` (ecstatic), `.uisai` (very happy), `.uiru'e` (slightly happy)
- Practice: `.uinai` (unhappy), `.uinaisai` (very unhappy)
- Practice: `.uicu'i` (indifferent)
- Drill: Create intensity scales for 5 emotions

## Day 4 — Attitudinal Placement (15 min)

- Practice placing attitudinals in different positions
- Compare: `.ui mi klama` vs. `mi .ui klama` vs. `mi klama .ui`
- Understand scope: what does the attitudinal modify?
- Practice: `mi .uu viska lo .oi gerku` (I (pity) see a (pain!) dog)
- Drill: Place attitudinals in 3 different positions in 5 sentences

## Day 5 — Evidentials (15 min)

- Learn ba'a, ti'e, za'a, pe'i, ju'o
- Practice: `za'a do klama` (I observe you going)
- Practice: `ti'e la .tam. cu snada` (I heard Tom succeeded)
- Practice: `pe'i ti xamgu` (in my opinion, this is good)
- Practice: `ju'o ti jetnu` (certainly this is true)
- Drill: Report 5 facts with different evidentials

## Day 6 — Combining Attitudinals & Evidentials (15 min)

- Combine emotions with evidence
- Practice: `.ui za'a do klama` (Happy! I observe you going)
- Practice: `ti'e .uu la .tam. cu terpa` (I heard (pity!) Tom is afraid)
- Practice: `pe'i .ie ti xamgu` (In my opinion (I agree), this is good)
- Drill: Create 5 complex sentences with both types

## Day 7 — Conversation Practice (15 min)

- React to 10 different scenarios with appropriate attitudinals
- Practice expressing certainty vs. doubt: ju'o vs. pe'i
- Practice reporting sources: ti'e vs. za'a
- Self-test: Given 10 neutral sentences, add emotions and evidentials
- Review: Full intensity scale for 3 common attitudinals
- Final practice: Tell a story using at least 8 different attitudinals/evidentials
- Reflection: How attitudinals replace tone of voice in speech
