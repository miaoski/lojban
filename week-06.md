# Week 06 — `se` — Your First Conversion Operator

## 1. Vocabulary to Learn (8 gismu)

- **ponse** — own
- **pleji** — pay
- **pilno** — use
- **canja** — exchange
- **ralte** — keep
- **bevri** — carry
- **muvdu** — move
- **lebna** — take

## 2. Grammar to Learn

- **`se` operator:** Swaps x1 and x2 of any selbri
- **Example conversion:**
  - `tavla`: x1 talks to x2 about x3
  - `se tavla`: x1 is talked to by x2 about x3
- **Why it matters:** The x1 position is the topic/focus of the sentence. `se` lets you foreground a different participant.
- **`se dunda`:** x1 is given x2 by x3 (the recipient becomes the focus)
- **Practice pattern:** Take a bridi, rewrite with `se`, compare what's foregrounded

## 3. Vocabulary to Refresh

- **Week 1:** prami, klama, tavla, citka, pinxe, cadzu, ciska, tcidu, sipna, melbi, xamgu, xlali
- **Week 2:** cmene, prenu, nanmu, ninmu, verba, zdani, sutra, barda
- **Week 3:** cmalu, gleki, badri, nelci, djica, gerku, mlatu, plise
- **Week 4:** dunda, vecnu, lebna, bajra, viska, tirna, penmi, djuno, cusku, benji
- **Week 5:** cukta, skami, stizu, jubme, kumfa, dinju, karce, jdima, zarci, rupnu

## 4. Grammar to Refresh

- Place structure (x1, x2, x3...)
- `zo'e` for unspecified places
- Multi-place predicates (2, 3, 4, 5 places)
- Left-to-right filling

## 5. Conversation Topics

- "The book is given by me"
- "The car is used by Alice"
- Passive-like constructions using `se`
- Foregrounding different participants

**Example sentence:** `la .cukta. se dunda mi` — The book is given by me.

## Daily Breakdown

### Day 1 — Understanding `se`

- Learn: `se` swaps x1 and x2
- Compare: `mi prami do` vs `do se prami mi` (same meaning, different focus)
- Practice with viska: `mi viska la cukta` vs `la cukta se viska mi`
- Create 5 pairs showing the same relationship both ways

### Day 2 — `se` with Two-Place Predicates

- Practice: `mi nelci la plise` → `la plise se nelci mi`
- Practice: `mi viska la gerku` → `la gerku se viska mi`
- Learn: pilno (use) — `mi pilno la skami` → `la skami se pilno mi`
- Create 6 sentences, then convert each with `se`

### Day 3 — `se dunda` — Foregrounding the Recipient

- Original: dunda (x1 gives x2 to x3)
- With `se`: se dunda (x1 is given x2 by x3)
- Practice: `mi dunda la cukta la .bob.` → `la .bob. se dunda la cukta mi`
- Notice how the recipient is now the topic
- Create 5 giving sentences, convert to `se dunda`

### Day 4 — New Vocabulary with `se`

- Learn: ponse (own), bevri (carry), muvdu (move)
- Practice: `mi ponse la karce` → `la karce se ponse mi` (The car is owned by me)
- Practice: `mi bevri la cukta` → `la cukta se bevri mi` (The book is carried by me)
- Create 6 sentences using new gismu with and without `se`

### Day 5 — `se` with Three-Place Predicates

- Focus on: tavla (x1 talks to x2 about x3)
- With `se`: se tavla (x1 is talked to by x2 about x3)
- Practice: `mi tavla la .bob. la cukta` → `la .bob. se tavla mi la cukta`
- Try with cusku: `mi cusku ti do` → `do se cusku ti mi`
- Create 5 three-place conversions

### Day 6 — Practical `se` Usage

- Learn: pleji (pay), canja (exchange), ralte (keep)
- Practice choosing when `se` is useful
- Example: To say "This book is owned by Alice", use `ti cukta cu se ponse la .alis.`
- Create 6 sentences where `se` makes the sentence more natural
- Focus on: what do you want to talk about first?

### Day 7 — Integration and Free Practice

- Take 5 gismu from Weeks 4-5
- Write their normal place structure
- Write their `se` conversion structure
- Create 10 sentences: 5 normal, 5 with `se`
- Practice recognizing when `se` changes the focus/topic
- Review: `se` is about foregrounding different participants, not changing meaning
